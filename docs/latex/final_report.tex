\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present a DirectX~9.0c fixed-function \textbf{sandbox} (not a scored game) set on procedural mountainous terrain. The user drives a textured vehicle with keyboard or XInput gamepad; a third-person camera follows the chassis. The terrain uses bilinear height sampling, slope-based texture splatting (grass/rock), and surface normals for vehicle orientation. Static props include a crate (AABB) and a loose boulder (bounding sphere with gravity and impulses). A windmill model is loaded through the DirectX frame hierarchy with \texttt{ID3DXAnimationController} when present in the asset. A simple day/night cycle modulates the sun direction, ambient light, and clear colour; an \texttt{ID3DXFont} HUD shows speed and position. The implementation is native Visual C++17 with CMake, satisfying the course requirements for real-time 3D, input, physics-based motion, navigation on geometry, collision, and Maya-exported animation integration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Completed application}
\label{sec:application}

\subsection{Feature overview}
Procedural terrain mesh with bilinear sampling and two-pass rock splatting; simple longitudinal vehicle dynamics with steering, rolling resistance, and braking; kinematic terrain following (pose from surface normal); chase camera; crate and boulder models with AABB/sphere collision; boulder gravity and impulse from the vehicle hull; hierarchical windmill draw with animation time stepped each frame; day/night lighting and clear colour; on-screen HUD (\texttt{ID3DXFont}); resize-safe device reset path for the font.

\section{Technical description}
\label{sec:technical}

This section summarizes the main techniques used in the DirectX~9.0c (fixed-function pipeline) implementation: terrain sampling, vehicle--terrain coupling, collision choices, animation, lighting, and environmental effects. The application is a physics sandbox (not a scored game).

\subsection{Architecture overview}
The real-time loop follows input $\rightarrow$ simulation $\rightarrow$ rendering: keyboard via \texttt{GetAsyncKeyState}, gamepad via \texttt{XInputGetState}; \texttt{Game::Update} integrates the vehicle and boulder, resolves collisions, advances animation time, and updates lighting parameters; \texttt{Game::Render} sets the chase camera, draws terrain, hierarchy model, props, and vehicle with the fixed-function pipeline and a font overlay.

\subsection{DirectX~9 rendering}
The renderer uses a Direct3D~9 device with a depth--stencil buffer, textured meshes, and directional lighting. Meshes and animations are loaded from the \texttt{.X} format; the coordinate system is left-handed, consistent with \texttt{D3DXMatrixLookAtLH} and standard DirectX samples.

\subsection{Terrain and model navigation}
\label{sec:techniques-terrain}

\subsubsection{Bilinear interpolation for terrain height}
The terrain height at an arbitrary world position $(x,z)$ is obtained from a heightmap grid. To avoid discrete ``stair-stepping'' when the vehicle moves, the engine samples the four neighbouring heightmap texels and applies \textbf{bilinear interpolation}:
\[
h(x,z) = (1-t_x)(1-t_z)\,h_{00} + t_x(1-t_z)\,h_{10} + (1-t_x)t_z\,h_{01} + t_x t_z\,h_{11},
\]
where $t_x,t_z \in [0,1]$ are fractional coordinates within the cell. This yields a continuous height field suitable for stable contact and visual alignment.

\subsubsection{Surface normal alignment}
The vehicle's up vector is aligned with the \textbf{terrain surface normal}, computed from the local geometry (e.g.\ cross product of edges on the heightfield triangle under the vehicle). Pitch and roll follow the normal while yaw is driven by steering input, giving kinematic terrain-following without penetrating the mesh.

\subsection{Animation}
\label{sec:techniques-animation}
\textbf{Maya keyframed} animation for the windmill is exported in the \texttt{.X} format. The runtime uses \texttt{D3DXLoadMeshHierarchyFromX} with an \texttt{ID3DXAllocateHierarchy} implementation (\texttt{src/AnimatedModel.cpp}) and advances \texttt{ID3DXAnimationController} each frame. The jeep mesh is drawn as a single subset from \texttt{D3DXLoadMeshFromX} without per-wheel hierarchy in this build.

\subsection{Collision detection and response}
\label{sec:techniques-collision}

\subsubsection{Axis-aligned bounding boxes (AABB)}
\textbf{Rectangular props} (e.g.\ wooden crates) use \textbf{AABB} tests: fast intersection in world space along $x$, $y$, and $z$ extents. Response can slide or push the vehicle along the minimum penetration axis.

\subsubsection{Bounding spheres}
\textbf{Irregular objects} (e.g.\ boulders) use \textbf{bounding sphere} collision: distance between sphere centres compared to the sum of radii. Spheres are cheap to update when objects rotate or roll and pair well with impulse-based response for loose rocks.

\subsubsection{Optional dynamic interaction}
Where implemented, \textbf{dynamic-to-dynamic} contact allows the vehicle to impart momentum to loose boulders so they roll on slopes and interact with other objects (see \S\ref{sec:bonus}).

\subsection{Physics and simulation}
\label{sec:techniques-physics}
Gravity is applied to the boulder as a constant downward acceleration (engine constant $\approx 28$ units/s$^2$ for stable stepping). The vehicle uses semi-implicit longitudinal integration with throttle, rolling resistance, and brake terms (\texttt{src/Vehicle.cpp}).

\subsection{Environmental effects}
A \textbf{day/night cycle} orbits the directional light and interpolates clear and ambient colours (\texttt{src/Game.cpp}). \textbf{Texture splatting} blends grass and rock in two passes using vertex alpha from slope. The HUD shows speed, world position, simulation time, and windmill load state.

\subsection{Bonus work}
\label{sec:bonus}
\textbf{Partial bonus:} the boulder is dynamic (gravity, velocity, sphere versus vehicle AABB and crate AABB with positional correction and simple impulse when hit by the vehicle). Full rigid-body stacking and boulder--boulder coupling are not implemented.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The sandbox meets the core Stage~3 requirements in a compact codebase. Known limitations: no audio, no skinned character pipeline, jeep wheels are not independently articulated in code, and terrain is procedural rather than artist-authored DEM data. Future work could import a real heightmap PNG, add wheel bones from hierarchy, and extend physics to full rigid-body contact.
